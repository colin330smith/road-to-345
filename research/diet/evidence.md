# Evidence: the strict home-cooked maintenance system

Written 2026-11-01 for the diet rebuild: MAINTAIN now (Cycles 5–6, Nov 9 – Jan 3, max test Fri Jan 1), the six-week TRIM from Mon Jan 4 if the tape says so, then GAIN, all on one home-cooked system. The new rule is zero Hillstone food. The rows use the EVIDENCE.md format and labels (Evidence / Partly / Judgment / Truth) and are written to sit under "Nutrition and recovery". "(above)" means a row earlier in this file or one already in EVIDENCE.md (Helms 2014, Garthe 2011, Morton 2018, Nedeltcheva 2010, Helms 2023).

**How it was checked.** Every citation was checked on 2026-11-01 against its PubMed record (citation, abstract, retraction and erratum notices), Europe PMC full text, the publisher's page or the official document. That covers the 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines PDF from realfood.gov, 21 CFR 101.9 through the eCFR API, live USDA FoodData Central records, NIAAA, FAO and MacroFactor's own pages. A retraction scan of all 68 PubMed IDs found none retracted. Barbalho is never cited. Orsama 2014 (a weekend weight rhythm) is left out because Brian Wansink is a co-author; Turicchi 2020 shows the same rhythm in 1,421 people. Every number about him was computed with node: `evidence-tools/calc.js`, output in `evidence-tools/calc-out.json`. The inputs were 191 lb (86.6 kg), 18–20% fat, 69–71 kg fat-free mass and 15.6–17.3 kg fat.

**What the evidence says**
1. **The counting failed, not the maths.** Self-report misses about 19% in athletes measured with doubly labelled water. Most of the miss is food never logged (condiments, tastes) and eyeballed portions. In the classic "diet-resistant" patients, the cause was intake well above what they reported; energy expenditure was within 5% of predicted. A restaurant that publishes no numbers is the worst case: restaurant meals measured by bomb calorimetry came to 1,205 ± 465 kcal. Zero Hillstone food removes the one stream he could never weigh.
2. **Strict means steady, not perfect.** A logging habit that misses the same amount every day calibrates itself. An unlogged staff meal twice a week, or a loose weekend, cannot be calibrated. Weight-loss maintainers who ate the same way all week were 1.5 times more likely to hold their weight over the next year.
3. **Maintenance is measured, not guessed.** It is the average logged intake minus the energy in the weight trend, over a rolling 28 days of complete logs and daily weigh-ins. At the likely scale noise (about 1.2 lb a day), 14 days pins it only to about ±280 kcal. 28 days pins it to about ±100–160 kcal. The first week after the switch to home food is water, glycogen and gut content, so it is left out. Old logs cannot set the new number.
4. **3,500 kcal per lb has limits.** It is wrong for long-range forecasts, and wrong for raw one- or two-week scale changes, which are mostly water. It is still a fair converter for a sustained small trend at his body fat. Hall's Forbes model puts his tissue change at 2,899–2,985 kcal/lb, about 20 kcal a day different at 0.25 lb/wk.
5. **Home-cooked, minimally processed, lower-energy-density food makes a fixed number easier to hold.** In the inpatient trial, people ate about 508 kcal a day more on ultra-processed food, yet rated both diets equally pleasant. A 2025 trial found twice the weight loss on minimally processed food that met the same guidelines. Adherence, not diet type, predicts the outcome, so restaurant-quality flavour belongs in the plan.
6. **Protein, fibre and drinks.** Protein at 190–200 g and fibre at about 14 g per 1,000 kcal help fullness. There are no liquid calories beyond the logged whey and the milk in recipes. Alcohol is 7 kcal per gram, does not displace food (people eat more with it) and blunts muscle protein synthesis at high doses.
7. **What maintenance in the peak protects.** It protects lean-mass gain and the waist. Strength gains held up even in an energy deficit in the meta-analysis, so the ban on trims in Cycles 5–6 stays a judgment, not a finding.

## Eating at work: the zero-Hillstone rule

| Plan element | Label | Source | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero Hillstone food: no staff meal, no tasting, no grazing; every bite is his own weighed food | Judgment | Urban 2016, *J Acad Nutr Diet* 116(4):590 · Urban 2011 (above) · Hollands 2019, *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 9:CD012573 · Robinson 2013, *Am J Clin Nutr* 97(4):728 · Onufrak 2019, *J Acad Nutr Diet* 119(6):999 · Hall 2011 (above) | No study tests a restaurant worker's staff-meal ban; the supporting evidence is indirect. By Hall's rule, a steady unlogged 100 kcal a day ends about 9 lb heavier, 4.6 lb of it in the first year. The rule removes the one food stream he cannot weigh. |
| Shift meals packed to travel and eaten on the break; the post-close meal planned and logged in advance | Partly | Vujović 2022, *Cell Metab* 34(10):1486 · Al Khatib 2017, *Eur J Clin Nutr* 71(5):614 | Late eating at matched calories raised hunger and lowered waking energy expenditure in adults with overweight (randomised crossover). ## Judgment calls in this section, in one list

1. The pace of strict logging: log before eating, one tap per prepped portion, a "day complete" tap, at least 6 of 7 complete days per week.
2. The maintenance estimate's thresholds: a 7-day run-in after a diet or mode change; 21 days for a provisional number and a 28-day rolling window; at least 5 weigh-ins a week; the ± band from his own residuals; the target moves only past the band and 100 kcal; rounded to 50 kcal; 3,500 kcal/lb as the converter.
3. The provisional MAINTAIN target until it is measured (the GAIN–TRIM midpoint, 2,775).
4. Protein 190–200 g at maintenance (a satiety margin above the muscle floor); 4–5 feedings of about 40–50 g.
5. The zero-Hillstone rule's details: shift-meal packing and timing, and the planned post-close meal.
6. The rotation: how many signature dishes, how many choices per slot, which staples repeat.
7. The whey exception to "no liquid calories"; the alcohol cap's count and timing.
8. The weigh-in protocol; holiday days on the same plan.
9. The no-trim rule in Cycles 5–6 (already a judgment in EVIDENCE.md): the evidence protects lean mass and the waist, not peak strength.

## What could not be verified

- His own scale noise. The ±kcal bands assume 1.0–1.5 lb (anchored to Turicchi 2020's 0.62–0.64% imputation error) and, in one line, a lag-1 correlation of 0.5. Both are assumptions until his residuals exist.
- Whether the Forbes split (and so about 2,900–3,000 kcal/lb) holds for a trained lifter at maintenance. The model was built on mostly untrained people.
- Hall 2019's eating-rate numbers and what its two errata corrected. The errata's existence was checked; their content was not, so neither is quoted.
- Olsson & Saltin 1970 (glycogen and water). PubMed has no abstract, so the 3–4 g figure is cited from Fernández-Elías 2015 and Kreitzman 1992 instead.
- MacroFactor's exact V3 formula, which is unpublished.
- The 2025–2030 Guidelines' release date (January 7, 2026) is from news reports. The recommendations quoted are from the official PDF.
- No study was found on restaurant workers' eating on shift, on logging before eating, or on monotony versus adherence for a fixed meal plan. Those rows are labelled Judgment or Partly.
